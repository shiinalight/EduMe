    Pythagoras’ theorem

    In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle. The area of the square on that side equals the combined areas of the squares on the two shorter sides.

    a² + b² = c²
    1. Identify the hypotenuse as c.
    2. Square each side length.
    3. Add the two shorter-side squares, then solve for c.

    For a 3–4–5 triangle: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, so c = 5.
